## VMware vs OpenStack: Broadcom's Acquisition Pushes Companies to Look Elsewhere

The IT world is in turmoil. Following the recent acquisition of VMware by Broadcom, many companies face a strategic crossroads: continue with an increasingly expensive licensing model or explore more flexible and sustainable alternatives? The question is no longer whether there is an alternative, but which one is the best. In this scenario, the **VMware vs OpenStack** comparison becomes crucial, not just as a technical choice, but as a business decision capable of **reducing Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) by up to 40%**.

OpenStack is not just software, but an open source cloud platform that allows you to build and manage private and public clouds. Its modular nature and the absence of license costs make it the most powerful alternative to VMware, especially for SMEs seeking efficiency and control over their infrastructure. A correct [evaluation of the transition to open source software](/en/solutions/open-source-software) is the first step to unlocking this potential.

## VMware vs OpenStack: The Decisive Comparison for Your Business

The choice between a proprietary ecosystem and an open source one directly impacts costs, flexibility, and innovation. Understanding the fundamental differences is essential for an informed decision.

| Feature | VMware vSphere | Canonical OpenStack |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Licensing Model** | Proprietary, per CPU/core/subscription | Open Source, cost based on support (optional) |
| **Cost (TCO)** | High, driven by software licenses | Up to 40% lower, zero license costs |
| **Vendor Lock-in** | High, strong interdependence between components | Low/None, freedom to choose components |
| **Architecture** | Monolithic and integrated (vSphere, vSAN, NSX) | Modular and composable (Ceph, OVN, etc.) |
| **Scalability** | Predominantly vertical (hardware upgrade) | Natively horizontal (adding servers) |
| **Flexibility** | Limited to the VMware ecosystem | Maximum, integration with best-of-breed |

## Why is a Migration from VMware to OpenStack a Strategic Move Now?

Beyond the changes in VMware's licensing policies, there are structural advantages that make OpenStack a winning choice for the future.

### 1. Drastic TCO Reduction: A Real Case
The most tangible advantage is economic. An analysis on a real case of a 150-node infrastructure demonstrates that, with equal hardware costs, **the TCO of a solution based on Canonical OpenStack can be up to 40% lower than VMware vSphere**. The savings do not come from less performant hardware, but from the complete elimination of software license costs, which in the VMware ecosystem can represent almost 50% of the total investment. This frees up valuable budget to reinvest in innovation.

### 2. Escape from Vendor Lock-in and Total Control
OpenStack's modular approach is its greatest strategic strength. You are not forced to use a single solution for storage (vSAN) or networking (NSX). You can choose the best technologies on the market, like **Ceph** for distributed storage and **OVN (Open Virtual Network)** for networking, and integrate them into a tailor-made architecture. This flexibility, at the center of every modern [IT architecture design](/en/solutions/it-architecture-design), gives you back full control over your infrastructure.

## \"But Will I Lose the Features I'm Used To?\" The Truth About Feature Parity

The most common fear in a migration is losing critical features. Fortunately, the OpenStack ecosystem offers mature and high-performance alternatives for almost every VMware component, allowing a *lift-and-shift* migration in most cases.

| VMware Component/Feature | Equivalent in OpenStack Ecosystem |
| :--- | :--- |
| **vSphere / vCenter Server** | OpenStack (with Horizon dashboard and Nova service) |
| **vSAN (Storage)** | Ceph (object, block, and file system storage) |
| **NSX (Networking & Security)** | OVN (Open Virtual Network) |
| **Tanzu Kubernetes Grid** | Canonical Kubernetes (integratable on-demand) |
| **vMotion (Live Migration)** | OpenStack Native Live Migration |
| **vCenter HA** | Native High Availability of OpenStack services |

## Migration is Not a Game: An Engineering Approach is Needed

If the advantages are clear, it is equally clear that a migration from VMware to OpenStack is a complex project that goes beyond simple software installation. It requires planning, architectural skills, and meticulous management to avoid service interruptions and ensure the achievement of business objectives. Relying on chance or non-specialized personnel can turn a savings opportunity into a costly failure.

## Conclusions: The Choice is Not Just Technical, It's Business

The **VMware vs OpenStack** debate today ends with a strong argument in favor of open source. Faced with rising costs and less control, OpenStack emerges as a mature, high-performance, and strategically advantageous solution, capable of guaranteeing flexibility and a significantly lower TCO. The real challenge is not *if* to migrate, but *how* to do it effectively.
